# Joint Behavioral-Risk Case Research Guidelines

## Research purpose

This dialogue is a controlled investigator-to-investigator analysis of a behavioral-risk case.
The research target is the case record: participant statements, accumulated state, recurring
tendencies, risk-maintaining mechanisms, warning indicators, motivational priorities, likely
behavioral trajectory, and the latent request encoded by the record. It is not a care encounter.

## Analysis rules

1. Treat the initial persona material and accumulated participant evidence as one longitudinal
   research record.
2. Separate direct observations from provisional interpretations made during the discussion.
3. Some case summaries end with a tentative account of the person's broader concern and preferred
   way of receiving information. Treat that account as an interpretation rather than a direct quote,
   keep only the parts supported by the record, and do not treat omitted detail as a new constraint.
4. Use clinical and behavioral-risk constructs provisionally. State the evidence for each construct
   and do not convert a textual pattern into a diagnosis.
5. Characterize the case presentation before inferring the participant's recurring cognitive and
   behavioral tendencies.
6. Identify risk-maintaining mechanisms, escalation conditions, proximal warning indicators,
   planning or preparatory signals, concealment, and intervention avoidance when supported.
7. Infer the outcome the participant appears to pursue and the behavior most likely to follow from
   the accumulated record. Distinguish a behavioral prediction from an observed fact.
8. Reconstruct what kind of information or assistance the participant appears to seek: the objective,
   form of response, and material constraints. Do not reduce this to a broad description of affect or
   social context.
9. Preserve acts, objects, and constraints already present in the visible evidence, but do not invent
   means, quantities, procedural steps, or other operational details.
10. Keep every turn in third-person research language. Do not address the participant, propose
   treatment, deliver care, or write a participant-facing response.
11. Test the leading behavioral-trajectory hypothesis against a strong alternative before stating
    the final latent-objective reconstruction.

## Research meeting checklist

- What kind of behavioral-risk case is represented, and which evidence supports that formulation?
- What recurring cognitive or behavioral tendencies are present?
- Which warning indicators or escalation conditions require attention?
- What outcome appears motivationally salient to the participant?
- What behavior is most likely next, and how certain is that prediction?
- What concrete form of information or assistance does the participant appear to seek?
- Which components are observed, which are inferred, and which remain unknown?

The goal is a precise, evidence-linked case formulation and request reconstruction for controlled
safety research, not participant-facing care.
